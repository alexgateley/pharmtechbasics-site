Question 1 · Medications

In a hospital outpatient pharmacy, a technician constitutes a bottle of Zyvox (linezolid) for oral suspension by adding 123 mL of distilled water in two portions, giving 150 mL at 100 mg/5 mL. Which storage and discard instruction belongs on the prescription label?

  1. Refrigerate at 2 to 8 °C; discard 14 days after mixing
  2. Room temperature; discard 14 days after mixing
  3. Refrigerate at 2 to 8 °C; discard 10 days after mixing
  4. Room temperature; discard 21 days after mixing

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. Tempting because many antibiotic suspensions are refrigerated with a 14-day limit, but linezolid suspension is kept at room temperature and lasts 21 days.
  • B. The storage condition is right, but the dating is a week short, which would have the patient throw out usable suspension before a 14-day or longer course is finished.
  • C. Tempting because a refrigerated 10-day limit is common for other antibiotic suspensions, but it does not match the linezolid label on either storage or dating.
  • D. Correct. The Zyvox label says to store the constituted suspension at room temperature and use it within 21 days.

Stability after constitution is set by each product's label, not by a house rule for antibiotics. The Zyvox label has the powder tapped loose and the 123 mL of water added in two portions with vigorous shaking, which yields 150 mL at 100 mg per 5 mL. Before each dose the bottle is mixed by gently inverting it 3 to 5 times rather than shaking, so an auxiliary label saying to invert gently, not shake, fits as well. The constituted suspension is stored at room temperature and used within 21 days, so the discard date is 21 days from the day it was mixed.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 2 · Medications

A long-term care pharmacy technician is building the medication administration record entry for a resident's new order: tamsulosin 0.4 mg capsule, one capsule by mouth daily. Which administration note belongs on the entry?

  1. Give before breakfast; may sprinkle capsule contents on applesauce
  2. Give 30 minutes after the same meal each day; swallow capsule whole
  3. Give at bedtime on an empty stomach; capsule may be opened
  4. Give with a full glass of water; keep resident upright 30 minutes

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Tempting because several extended-release capsules allow sprinkling, but tamsulosin capsules are not to be opened, and the label ties the dose to the same meal rather than to an empty stomach.
  • B. Correct. The tamsulosin label directs dosing about one-half hour after the same meal each day and states the capsules should not be crushed, chewed or opened.
  • C. Tempting because some alpha-blockers are started at bedtime to limit dizziness, but tamsulosin is timed to a meal, and its label says the capsule should not be opened.
  • D. Tempting because it sounds like a careful oral-dosing rule, but staying upright with a full glass of water is the bisphosphonate instruction (for example alendronate), not a tamsulosin requirement.

Tamsulosin is an alpha-1 blocker used for the signs and symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia. Its label recommends 0.4 mg once daily about 30 minutes after the same meal each day and says the capsules should not be crushed, chewed or opened, so the record should say to swallow the capsule whole. Keeping the meal the same matters because food changes how much drug is absorbed. If staff report that a resident cannot swallow capsules, the technician refers the question to the pharmacist rather than choosing an alternative.

Sources: Tamsulosin Hydrochloride Capsules label, Synthon (2025)

Question 3 · Medications

At a mail-order pharmacy, a technician enters a new prescription for Strattera 40 mg, one capsule by mouth daily for ADHD. Which generic name and control status under federal law should be entered?

  1. Lisdexamfetamine, Schedule II
  2. Atomoxetine, not controlled
  3. Guanfacine ER, not controlled
  4. Atomoxetine, Schedule II

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Lisdexamfetamine is Vyvanse, a Schedule II stimulant for ADHD; the schedule is right for that drug, but it is not the ingredient in Strattera.
  • B. Correct. Strattera is atomoxetine, a nonstimulant ADHD capsule, and its label states that atomoxetine is not a controlled substance, so it is entered without a DEA schedule.
  • C. Guanfacine extended-release is Intuniv, another nonstimulant ADHD drug that is not controlled; the status matches, but it is a tablet with a different ingredient.
  • D. The ingredient is right, but the schedule is borrowed from the stimulants: methylphenidate and lisdexamfetamine are Schedule II, which is why candidates assume every ADHD drug is.

Strattera is atomoxetine, a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor labeled for ADHD in adults and children 6 years and older and sold as capsules from 10 mg to 100 mg. Unlike methylphenidate and lisdexamfetamine, which 21 CFR 1308.12 lists in Schedule II, atomoxetine is not a controlled substance, so the federal controlled-substance limits on refills and transfers do not apply to it. The label carries a boxed warning on suicidal thoughts in pediatric patients, and a Medication Guide goes out with each fill.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed); 21 CFR Part 1308 (schedules of controlled substances)

Question 4 · Medications

In a hospital IV room, a technician pulls a single-dose vial for an order written as Invanz 1 g IV every 24 hours. Which generic vial should the technician select?

  1. Imipenem and cilastatin sodium
  2. Ertapenem sodium
  3. Cefepime hydrochloride
  4. Meropenem

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Imipenem with cilastatin (Primaxin IV) is another carbapenem vial in the same area, which makes it tempting, but it is not the generic of Invanz.
  • B. Correct. Invanz is ertapenem, a carbapenem supplied as a 1 g single-dose vial for IV infusion or IM injection.
  • C. Cefepime is a beta-lactam vial often stocked next to the carbapenems, but it is a cephalosporin, a different class from the drug in Invanz.
  • D. Meropenem is a carbapenem given IV in hospitals, so it looks like the right shelf, but it is a different drug and is usually dosed several times a day, not once.

Invanz is the brand of ertapenem, which its label calls a penem (carbapenem) antibacterial, supplied as a 1 g single-dose vial. Its preparation rules matter in the IV room: it must not be mixed or co-infused with other drugs, diluents containing dextrose must not be used, an adult IV dose is reconstituted with 10 mL of Water for Injection or 0.9% sodium chloride and added to 50 mL of 0.9% sodium chloride, and the infusion is finished within 6 hours of reconstitution. The other carbapenems look alike on the shelf, which is why the generic name is checked letter by letter.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 5 · Medications

At an urgent care pharmacy, a technician enters a prescription for nitrofurantoin macrocrystals 50 mg capsules: take 1 capsule by mouth four times daily with food for 7 days. Which quantity and days supply should the technician enter?

  1. 56 capsules for a 7-day supply
  2. 28 capsules for a 14-day supply
  3. 14 capsules for a 7-day supply
  4. 28 capsules for a 7-day supply

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. This doubles the dose, as if each dose were two capsules; the sig calls for 1 capsule per dose.
  • B. The quantity is right, but 14 days doubles the days supply, so the claim and the next refill date would run past the 7-day course.
  • C. This is the count for Macrobid (monohydrate/macrocrystals), taken as 100 mg every 12 hours for 7 days; the macrocrystals product in this prescription is taken four times daily.
  • D. Correct. Four doses a day for 7 days is 4 × 7 = 28 capsules, and the course the prescriber wrote lasts 7 days.

Nitrofurantoin comes in two oral capsule products that are easy to confuse at data entry. Nitrofurantoin macrocrystals is labeled at 50 to 100 mg four times a day, with the lower dose recommended for uncomplicated urinary tract infections, and therapy usually runs one week. Macrobid, the monohydrate/macrocrystals product, is labeled at one 100 mg capsule every 12 hours for seven days. Here the sig gives 4 capsules a day × 7 days = 28 capsules and a 7-day supply, so the claim and the refill date follow the 7-day course. Both labels say to take the drug with food because food improves absorption and tolerance.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 6 · Federal Requirements

A technician at a community pharmacy that is also a DEA-authorized collector with a take-back receptacle helps take the biennial controlled substance inventory. Under federal law, which of these must be listed on the inventory as a counted drug quantity?

  1. Pseudoephedrine 30 mg tablets kept behind the counter
  2. Each drug inside the sealed take-back receptacle liners
  3. Gabapentin 300 mg capsules in the regular stock
  4. Expired oxycodone awaiting the reverse distributor

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. Tempting because pseudoephedrine sales are logged under federal law, but it is a List I chemical, not a scheduled controlled substance, so it is not on the controlled substance inventory.
  • B. Tempting because the liners hold controlled substances, but for a collection receptacle the inventory records the number, size and identification number of the liners, not a count of their contents.
  • C. Tempting because several states control gabapentin, but it is not in any federal schedule, so the federal inventory does not include it.
  • D. Correct. Damaged, expired or otherwise unusable controlled substances still on hand, such as stock waiting for a reverse distributor, belong on the inventory with the quantity and the reason the pharmacy is holding them.

An inventory is a complete list of the controlled substances in the registrant's possession on the inventory date (21 CFR 1304.11). Damaged, defective or expired substances awaiting disposal are listed with the substance name, the total quantity or number of units, and the reason the pharmacy is holding them; they stay in the pharmacy's records until the reverse distributor takes them. A pharmacy that collects through a take-back receptacle inventories its unused and sealed inner liners by number, size and unique identification number, without opening them. List I chemicals and drugs controlled only by a state are outside the federal inventory.

Sources: 21 CFR Part 1304 (controlled substance records and inventories); DEA Pharmacist's Manual (2022); 21 CFR Part 1308 (schedules of controlled substances)

Question 7 · Federal Requirements

At a busy community pharmacy, the pharmacist-in-charge, who holds the store's CSOS digital certificate, asks a technician to submit tonight's Schedule II order by logging in with the pharmacist's certificate and password. Under federal rules, what is the BEST response?

  1. Leave the order for the pharmacist to sign with that certificate in person
  2. Fax the order to the wholesaler so it can be keyed in on their end
  3. Print a Form 222 and sign the pharmacist's name on the signature line
  4. Sign it with the certificate, since the certificate holder approved it

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Only the certificate holder may access or use his or her digital certificate and private key, so the pharmacist must sign the order personally (21 CFR 1311.30(a)).
  • B. A faxed list is not a valid Schedule II order; the only lawful orders are a signed Form 222 or a CSOS order signed with the purchaser's own DEA-issued certificate.
  • C. A Form 222 must be signed by the registrant or a person holding power of attorney; signing another person's name is not an authorized signature on either a paper or an electronic order.
  • D. Permission does not help: the rules require the holder to ensure no one else uses the private key, and a shared password is a compromise that must be reported for revocation.

CSOS orders are signed with a digital certificate tied to one person, who must keep the private key secure, may not let anyone else use it, and must report its loss or compromise to the certification authority within 24 hours. A certificate can be issued to the person who signed the registration application, a person authorized to sign one, or a person granted power of attorney to sign orders. A technician who will place Schedule II orders regularly therefore needs a power of attorney and a CSOS certificate of their own, which federal rules allow; until then the holder signs.

Sources: DEA: Controlled Substance Ordering System (CSOS); DEA Pharmacist's Manual (2022); 21 CFR Part 1305 (DEA Form 222 and CSOS orders)

Question 8 · Federal Requirements

A hospital pharmacy's hazardous waste profile lists expired cyclophosphamide as U058 and expired dalfampridine extended-release tablets as P008. Under federal EPA rules, what does the P code tell a technician sorting outdates about the dalfampridine?

  1. It is an acute hazardous waste, with stricter limits
  2. It is a partly used drug, while U marks unused stock
  3. It must be incinerated, while U waste may go to a landfill
  4. It is ignitable or corrosive, while U marks a toxic drug

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Under 40 CFR 261.33, P-listed discarded drugs are acute hazardous wastes and U-listed ones are toxic, non-acute wastes; acute waste is regulated at smaller amounts and with stricter empty-container rules.
  • B. Partial versus unused is a common memory aid, but it is wrong: both lists cover unused commercial chemical products whose listed chemical is the sole active ingredient.
  • C. The letter does not set the disposal method; both P- and U-listed wastes leave on a hazardous waste manifest for a permitted treatment facility.
  • D. Ignitability and corrosivity are characteristics with D codes (D001, D002), set by the waste's properties; the P and U lists name specific chemicals.

EPA lists discarded commercial chemical products in 40 CFR 261.33. The P list holds acute hazardous wastes, such as warfarin above 0.3% (P001), arsenic trioxide (P012), nicotine (P075) and 4-aminopyridine, the chemical name of dalfampridine (P008). The U list holds toxic, non-acute wastes, such as cyclophosphamide (U058), mitomycin (U010) and lindane (U129). The letter matters because acute waste is controlled in smaller amounts: outside Subpart P, more than 1 kg a month of acute waste makes a facility a large quantity generator, and a P-listed container must be triple rinsed to count as empty; Subpart P waste no longer counts toward generator category.

Sources: EPA: Management of Hazardous Waste Pharmaceuticals; FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 9 · Patient Safety and QA

A prescription reads: Nitrostat 0.4 mg, dissolve 1 tablet under the tongue at the first sign of chest pain. At data entry, the technician selects Nitrolingual (nitroglycerin lingual spray, 400 mcg per spray), and the label prints: "Spray 1 spray under the tongue at the first sign of chest pain." Which type of prescription error does this entry contain?

  1. Wrong route of administration
  2. Wrong strength on the label
  3. Wrong dosage form
  4. Wrong drug selected

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Tempting, because a tablet and a spray are used differently, but both are given under the tongue: the Nitrolingual label says to spray onto or under the tongue, and the printed directions keep that route.
  • B. The spray's 400 mcg per spray equals the prescribed 0.4 mg, so the dose matches. The unit change from mg to mcg is what makes this option look right.
  • C. Correct. The prescriber ordered sublingual tablets, and the entry is a metered lingual spray. The drug, the 0.4 mg dose and the under-the-tongue route all match, so the form is what changed.
  • D. Both products contain nitroglycerin, the drug prescribed. A wrong-drug error would be a different active ingredient, such as a look-alike name picked from the drug list.

A dosage form error is the right drug at the right dose in a form the prescriber did not order. Nitrostat is a sublingual tablet placed under the tongue or in the buccal pouch and allowed to dissolve without swallowing; Nitrolingual is a lingual aerosol that delivers 400 mcg per metered spray onto or under the tongue and must not be inhaled. Because the dose and route match, this error is easy to miss, so the technician compares the dosage form on the prescription, in the system and on the stock product. Changing a patient from tablets to a spray needs a new order from the prescriber, which the pharmacist handles.

Sources: DailyMed: Nitrostat (nitroglycerin) sublingual tablets label (2023); FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed); PTCB PTCE Content Outline (effective January 6, 2026)

Question 10 · Patient Safety and QA

In a long-term care pharmacy, a technician finishes counting azathioprine 50 mg tablets for a patient's 30-day blister card on the tray kept for hazardous drugs. Several tablets broke, and powder is left on the tray and on the counter beside it. Which cleanup method is BEST?

  1. Blow the tray clean with canned air over the hazardous waste bin
  2. Wet-wipe both surfaces in chemotherapy gloves and bag the wipes
  3. Sweep the powder into the hazardous waste bin with a dry paper towel
  4. Vacuum the counter with the pharmacy's regular shop vacuum

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Compressed air throws drug powder into the air the technician and coworkers breathe, which is the exposure the cleanup is meant to prevent.
  • B. Correct. Wet wiping picks up the powder without lifting it into the air, and sealing the used wipes in a bag keeps the residue contained for disposal.
  • C. The waste bin is right, but dry sweeping scatters fine powder and leaves residue on the counter, so it is not the best method.
  • D. NIOSH mentions HEPA-filtered vacuums for hazardous drug areas; an ordinary vacuum can blow fine drug particles back out through its exhaust.

Azathioprine is an immunosuppressant in Table 1 of the NIOSH 2024 hazardous drug list because it is classified as a human carcinogen, and its label carries a boxed warning about malignancy. NIOSH guidance for routine cleaning of hazardous drug residue is to use wet wiping methods, avoid creating dust, wear protective gloves and a gown, and place the used materials in sealed bags for disposal. Broken tablets release powder that can be inhaled or carried on hands, so methods that lift dust into the air, such as canned air, dry sweeping or an unfiltered vacuum, spread the exposure instead of removing it.

Sources: NIOSH List of Hazardous Drugs in Healthcare Settings, 2024; FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 11 · Patient Safety and QA

A handwritten prescription brought to a community pharmacy is read at intake as 'Klonopin 0.1 mg, 1 tablet twice daily, #60.' The patient's profile lists hypertension. What should the technician do?

  1. Ask the patient which drug was meant and enter that one
  2. Enter clonazepam 0.5 mg, the lowest Klonopin tablet made
  3. Refer it to the pharmacist to clarify with the prescriber
  4. Enter clonidine 0.1 mg, since the strength and the profile fit

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. The patient may know the purpose but cannot confirm the prescriber's intent, and a patient expecting a benzodiazepine could steer the answer.
  • B. Klonopin starts at 0.5 mg, five times the written figure. Rounding to the nearest strength would turn an unclear order into a different dose of a controlled drug.
  • C. Correct. The written strength fits clonidine, not Klonopin, so the drug itself is in doubt, and only the prescriber can say which was meant.
  • D. The fit is real, but deciding which drug a prescriber meant is a judgment for the pharmacist and the prescriber, not something a technician settles from the profile.

Klonopin (clonazepam, a Schedule IV benzodiazepine) is made as 0.5, 1 and 2 mg tablets, while clonidine, a centrally acting antihypertensive, usually starts at 0.1 mg twice daily. The names look and sound alike, and a strength that exists for only one of them is a classic clue that the wrong name was written or read. The technician does not pick a drug: the prescription goes to the pharmacist, who confirms with the prescriber, which matters because one choice is a controlled substance and the other is not.

Sources: ISMP Confused Drug Names List (2024); FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 12 · Patient Safety and QA

A mail-order technician is preparing a shipment of Humulin R U-500 20 mL vials. The patient's last order included U-100 insulin syringes. Which syringe does the U-500 vial labeling call for?

  1. A U-100 insulin syringe, with the dose divided by 5
  2. A U-500 insulin syringe marked in units
  3. A 1 mL tuberculin syringe, with the dose measured in mL
  4. A U-100 insulin syringe, drawn up to the prescribed units

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Dividing by 5 is a hand conversion that has led to dosing errors. The labeling calls for a dedicated syringe, so no arithmetic stands between the patient and an overdose.
  • B. Correct. The vial instructions say to inject U-500 with a U-500 syringe, whose markings already account for 500 units/mL, so no conversion is needed.
  • C. The label lists tuberculin syringes among those that can give the wrong dose with U-500, because the dose must be converted to a volume by hand.
  • D. This is the error the profile invites. Drawing U-500 to the unit marks of a U-100 syringe delivers five times the intended dose.

Humulin R U-500 holds 500 units/mL, five times the concentration of U-100 insulin, which makes it a high-alert product: a mismatch between the insulin and the syringe multiplies the dose. The labeling tells patients to use only a U-500 syringe with the vial and warns that U-100, tuberculin or allergy syringes can cause a dangerous overdose. The KwikPen needs no syringe, and insulin must not be drawn from the pen into one. Before shipping, the technician confirms that the order carries U-500 syringes and alerts the pharmacist if it does not.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed); ISMP High-Alert Medications List (2024)

Question 13 · Order Entry and Processing

A hospital pharmacy receives a manufacturer's Class II recall letter for mycophenolate mofetil 500 mg tablets. The letter gives the product's NDC and lists two affected lots. When checking the shelf, which detail on each package should the technician match to the letter to find the recalled units?

  1. The lot number on the label
  2. The package code segment of the NDC
  3. The expiration date on the label
  4. The labeler code segment of the NDC

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. A recall is usually limited to specific lots, and the lot number ties each package to the batch it came from.
  • B. The package code tells bottle size or type, and it stays the same from one lot to the next, so it cannot separate affected lots.
  • C. Several lots can share an expiration month, so matching the date could pull good stock or miss a recalled package.
  • D. The labeler code identifies the firm, which is shared by every product that firm lists, so it is far too broad for a lot recall.

Under 21 CFR 7.49, a recall communication should identify the product, size, lot numbers, codes or serial numbers clearly enough for immediate identification, and should say what to do with the product. The NDC is the same for every lot of a product and package, so it confirms the product but not the batch. The technician matches lot numbers, removes the affected packages, quarantines them where they cannot be dispensed, and follows the letter's instructions for return and response. A Class II recall means use may cause temporary or medically reversible harm.

Sources: 21 CFR Part 7, Subpart C (recalls); FDA: Recalls Background and Definitions

Question 14 · Order Entry and Processing

A community pharmacy ships expired lorazepam 1 mg tablets and tramadol 50 mg tablets, both Schedule IV, to a DEA-registered reverse distributor for destruction, and keeps its own record of the transfer. Under federal law, which party submits DEA Form 41 for these drugs, and when?

  1. The pharmacy, on the day the carrier picks them up
  2. The reverse distributor, after it destroys them
  3. The reverse distributor, on the day it receives them
  4. The pharmacy, once the reverse distributor confirms destruction

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Tempting because the pharmacy is giving the drugs up, but DEA says Form 41 should not be used to record a transfer between a pharmacy and a reverse distributor.
  • B. Correct. Form 41 reports destruction, so the registrant that actually destroys the drugs submits it once they have been destroyed.
  • C. The right party but the wrong moment: receipt is a transfer, and Form 41 is the inventory of drugs surrendered for destruction, filed after they are destroyed.
  • D. The timing is closer, but the pharmacy did not destroy the drugs; its own documentation is the record of distribution it already keeps.

When a pharmacy sends controlled substances to a reverse distributor, the transfer and the destruction are documented separately. For Schedule III to V drugs the pharmacy keeps a record of distribution listing drug name, dosage form, strength, quantity and date transferred (21 CFR 1317.10(a)), readily retrievable for two years; for Schedule II the reverse distributor issues a Form 222. DEA Form 41, the Registrants Inventory of Drugs Surrendered, is submitted by the registrant that destroys the drugs, after destruction (21 CFR 1304.21(e)). A pharmacy files Form 41 itself only for drugs it destroys on site.

Sources: DEA Pharmacist's Manual (2022); 21 CFR Part 1317 (controlled substance disposal); 21 CFR Part 1304 (controlled substance records and inventories)

Question 15 · Order Entry and Processing

A mail-order pharmacy technician is packing 28-day supplies of injectable pens for four patients. The pharmacy adds a box of pen needles to an order unless the pen carton already contains needles for the doses in that pen. Which order can ship without a separate box of pen needles?

  1. Victoza pen, 1.8 mg once daily
  2. Humalog KwikPen, 8 units with meals
  3. Lantus SoloStar, 30 units once daily
  4. Ozempic 1 mg pen, 1 mg once weekly

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. Tempting because Victoza is another Novo Nordisk GLP-1 pen, but its carton states that pen needles are not included.
  • B. Tempting because KwikPens are ready to use, but the carton says needles are not included, and three daily injections need many needles.
  • C. Tempting because SoloStar is a prefilled disposable pen, but the label says needles are not included and are sold separately.
  • D. Correct. Each Ozempic 1 mg pen carton holds one pen with 4 doses and 4 NovoFine Plus needles, so a 28-day supply already includes a needle for each injection.

Pen needles are a separate prescription item for most insulin and GLP-1 pens, so the technician checks each carton's contents before packing. The Ozempic multi-dose pens include NovoFine Plus needles: 6 with the 0.25/0.5 mg starter pen and 4 with each 1 mg or 2 mg pen, one per weekly dose. Victoza, Lantus SoloStar and Humalog KwikPen cartons state that needles are not included. A new needle is used for every injection, and pens are never shared between patients, even with a new needle.

Sources: OZEMPIC (semaglutide) injection label (Novo Nordisk, 2026); FDA Label: Lantus (insulin glargine) (2025); HUMALOG (insulin lispro) Prescribing Information; FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)
