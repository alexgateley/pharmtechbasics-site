Medications

A community pharmacy patient's profile shows Ativan (lorazepam) 1 mg tablets twice daily for anxiety from a psychiatrist. The patient's primary care provider is choosing a bedtime medicine for insomnia. Which of these products would duplicate the drug class of Ativan?

  1. Rozerem 8 mg
  2. Seroquel XR 50 mg
  3. Restoril 15 mg
  4. Remeron SolTab 15 mg

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Rozerem (ramelteon) is a melatonin receptor agonist for insomnia and is not a controlled substance, so it does not duplicate a benzodiazepine.
  • B. Seroquel XR (quetiapine) is an atypical antipsychotic that is sometimes prescribed for sleep, but it belongs to a different class from lorazepam.
  • C. Correct. Restoril is temazepam, a Schedule IV benzodiazepine like lorazepam, so adding it at bedtime duplicates the class.
  • D. Remeron (mirtazapine) is a sedating tetracyclic antidepressant, which makes it tempting for sleep, but it is not a benzodiazepine.

Lorazepam and temazepam are both benzodiazepines in federal Schedule IV. Both labels carry boxed warnings on profound sedation and respiratory depression with opioids, on abuse, misuse and addiction, and on dependence and withdrawal, and two benzodiazepines from two prescribers add to each of those risks. Ramelteon, mirtazapine and quetiapine are sedating but act on other receptors. When the second benzodiazepine arrives, the technician enters it with the duplicate-therapy alert so the pharmacist can review it and contact the prescribers.

Medications

A hospital discharge prescription is for Topamax Sprinkle Capsules 25 mg twice daily for a patient who has trouble swallowing capsules. Which administration instruction belongs on the prescription label?

  1. Sprinkle a day's doses on food and refrigerate the rest
  2. Open into a full glass of water and stir until dissolved
  3. Swallow whole, or sprinkle on soft food and swallow without chewing
  4. Chew the capsule contents well

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Tempting as a time saver for a caregiver, but the label says the drug and food mixture should be swallowed right away and not stored for later use.
  • B. Tempting because opening the capsule is allowed, but the label directs sprinkling on soft food, not dissolving the contents in water.
  • C. Correct. The label allows the capsule to be swallowed whole or opened and its contents sprinkled on a teaspoon of soft food, which is swallowed right away and not chewed.
  • D. Tempting because chewing seems to help a patient who cannot swallow capsules, but the label says the sprinkled mixture is swallowed without chewing.

Topamax Sprinkle Capsules (topiramate 15 mg and 25 mg) are made for patients who cannot swallow tablets. The label gives two options: swallow the capsule whole, or open it and sprinkle the contents on a small amount (about a teaspoon) of soft food, then swallow the mixture right away without chewing. The mixture is not kept for a later dose. Topiramate is labeled for epilepsy and for migraine prevention. The technician applies the matching auxiliary label, and the pharmacist explains the sprinkle technique to the patient or caregiver.

Patient Safety and QA

At a community pharmacy, a technician has just counted 30 amoxicillin 500 mg capsules on the counting tray, and a little capsule powder is left on the tray and spatula. The next prescription in the queue is for a patient whose profile lists a penicillin allergy with hives. What should the technician do BEFORE counting that order?

  1. Count on the same tray, since amoxicillin is not a NIOSH hazardous drug
  2. Wipe the tray and spatula with 70% isopropyl alcohol and let them dry
  3. Brush the powder off into the trash with a dry paper towel
  4. Rinse the tray under tap water and count the next order on it while wet

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Amoxicillin is not on the NIOSH list, but the reason to clean here is cross-contamination: penicillin residue can trigger a reaction in a patient with a penicillin allergy.
  • B. Correct. Alcohol on a lint-free wipe lifts the remaining powder, and letting the surfaces dry keeps moisture off the next patient's tablets.
  • C. Tempting because the tray looks clean afterward, but dry brushing leaves fine residue in the corners and on the spatula, which can still reach an allergic patient's tablets.
  • D. Water may remove some powder, but counting on a wet tray softens and sticks tablets and leaves moisture in the vial, so the tray must be dry before the next count.

Counting trays and spatulas are shared equipment, so dust from one drug can end up in the next patient's vial. Residue matters most with drugs that cause allergic reactions, such as penicillins and sulfonamides, because a trace amount can be enough to trigger a reaction in a sensitive patient. The amoxicillin label warns that serious, occasionally fatal hypersensitivity reactions occur and are more likely in people with a penicillin allergy. Wiping the tray and spatula with 70% isopropyl alcohol and letting them air dry removes the powder and leaves a dry surface for the next count.

Federal Requirements

A long-term care pharmacy mailed a DEA Form 222 for Focalin (dexmethylphenidate) 10 mg tablets, 100-count bottles, to its wholesaler two weeks ago. The wholesaler says it never received the form, and nothing has shipped. Under federal law, how should the pharmacy replace the order?

  1. Send the wholesaler a photocopy of the pharmacy's copy
  2. Send a new form with a statement on the lost form
  3. Ask the wholesaler to fill the order from a phone call
  4. Report the lost form to DEA on Form 106 first

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Tempting because the pharmacy kept a copy of the form it sent, but the supplier fills only the original Form 222, and a copy cannot stand in for a lost original.
  • B. Correct. The purchaser executes a new Form 222 and attaches a statement giving the order form number and date of the lost form and stating that the goods were not received because that form was lost; a copy of the statement goes to the supplier with the new form.
  • C. Tempting because it would restore supply quickly, but a Schedule II order between registrants needs a Form 222 or a CSOS order; a phone request cannot replace one.
  • D. Tempting because Form 106 is DEA's loss report, but it covers controlled substances that are stolen or lost, not an order form lost in the mail; a Form 222 lost in transit is replaced with a new form and a statement.

Under 21 CFR 1305.16(a), when an unfilled Form 222 is lost, the purchaser executes another one and attaches a statement with the order form number and date of the lost form, saying the goods it covered were not received because of the loss. The pharmacy keeps a copy of the new form and the statement with its copy of the first form, and if the lost form later reaches the supplier, the supplier marks it 'Not accepted' and returns it. Blank forms stolen or lost at the pharmacy are handled differently: they are reported at once to the DEA Special Agent in Charge with their serial numbers.
