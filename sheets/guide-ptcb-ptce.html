Exam tip Math is not only in Domain 4. The outline marks nine statements across all four domains as calculation-based, from doses and days supply to controlled substance quantities.
Exam tip Medications is the largest domain at 35%. Know brand and generic pairs, class and main use of common drugs before anything else.

What changed in January 2026

  • Removed: nonsterile compounding.
  • Removed: alligations.
  • Removed: narrow therapeutic index (NTI) drugs as their own area. They can still come up elsewhere when relevant.
  • Added: the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA): serialization, tracing, verification and quarantine.
  • Medications and Patient Safety shrank; Federal Requirements and Order Entry grew.
  • Same four domains, same total length.
Exam tip Books, apps and practice tests written before 2026 may still teach compounding and alligation for the PTCE, and may skip DSCSA. Study DSCSA. (The ExCPT still tests compounding and alligation.)

How to study: 6 weeks

  1. Week 1: Read the content outline. Set your exam date in Settings. Take a timed Mock Exam (90 items, 110 minutes) as a baseline. Start the drug cards by class.
  2. Week 2: Finish Medications: brand and generic names, indications, side effects, interactions, storage and stability (the storage, recalls and returns page helps). Daily Review every day from here on.
  3. Week 3: Federal Requirements with the DEA Schedules, CMEA, DSCSA, FDA recalls and REMS, and hazardous drugs pages.
  4. Week 4: Patient Safety with the Medication Safety and Error Prevention and OBRA 90 pages. Learn which issues go to the pharmacist.
  5. Week 5: Order Entry with the sig codes and pharmacy math pages and the Days Supply and Dilution calculators. Then NDC (Prescription Intake page), lot numbers, expiration dates and returns.
  6. Week 6: A second Mock Exam, then Weakest Subject, Missed Questions and Trouble Questions. Rest the day before.
Exam tip You get about 73 seconds per item. The app Mock Exam uses the real sitting: 90 items in 110 minutes.

Test-day tips

  • Arrive at the Pearson VUE center 30 minutes before your appointment.
  • Bring an approved ID that matches your name as it appears in your PTCB Account. Without proper ID you cannot test.
  • Try Pearson's PTCB exam tutorial at home so the screen is familiar.
  • Unscored items look like scored ones. Give every item full effort.
  • Do not let one hard item eat your time. Choose your best answer and keep moving.
  • Answer every item: a blank cannot earn credit.
  • Check the PTCB Help Center for the ID list and the rules on breaks, calculators and personal items.

Technician or pharmacist?

Area 3.3 is about recognizing issues that need pharmacist intervention. The exam expects the technician to notice the problem and bring it to the pharmacist, not to solve it.

The technicianThe pharmacist
Notices a DUR alert during entry and flags itReviews the alert and decides what to do
Hears a patient ask which cold medicine to buyMakes the OTC recommendation
Records a reported reaction or allergyJudges the adverse event and any change in therapy
Sees refill dates that suggest overuse or missed dosesAddresses misuse or adherence with the patient and prescriber
Passes on a request to switch to a different drugMakes the therapeutic substitution decision
Gets the patient to the pharmacist after a vaccine if they feel unwellHandles post-immunization care
Exam tip When an answer choice has the technician counseling, recommending a product or changing therapy, it is usually wrong. The best answer refers to the pharmacist.

Eligibility and applying

  • Live in the United States or its territories.
  • Pathway 1: a PTCB-recognized education/training program, completed or to be completed within 60 days of submitting your application. A pharmacy degree is accepted in place of the program.
  • Pathway 2: at least 500 hours of work experience as a pharmacy technician, completed by the time you apply.
  • Disclose all criminal history and any State Board of Pharmacy registration or licensure actions, follow PTCB policies, and pass the PTCE.
  • To apply: create a PTCB Account, apply through it, and wait for the email that authorizes you to schedule.
  • Need testing accommodations? Request them when you apply. PTCB gives ADA-consistent accommodations at no extra cost.

PTCB certification is national. Your state's board of pharmacy sets its own rules for technician registration or licensure, and they may require more than certification.

Results, retakes, emergencies and renewal

  • Results: the unofficial result shows on screen after the survey and can be printed at the center. The official result posts to your PTCB Account within three weeks; PTCB cannot speed this up.
  • Retakes: after four attempts, PTCB must review and approve a preparation activity before you can apply again: at least six months of tutoring by a pharmacist or PTCB CPhT (with the signed attestation form), a formal review course or training program, an ASHP/ACPE-accredited program, or a pharmacy technician associate degree. Activities finished before your most recent attempt do not count. The Help Center gives the waiting time between attempts.
  • Emergency on exam day (for example serious illness or injury, hospitalization, a death in the immediate family, a serious accident, a court appearance): send the Emergency Withdrawal form with documentation no later than 48 hours after the last day of your authorization period. You can ask to reschedule or for a refund; sponsored candidates get no refund.
  • Renewal: CPhTs recertify every 2 years. The recertification fee is $55; the CE hour requirements are in the Help Center.
