DSCSA: Tracing, Verification and Quarantine

What the Drug Supply Chain Security Act asks of a pharmacy: authorized partners, tracing documents, the product identifier, suspect and illegitimate product, the dates that apply now, and receiving

The law in brief

The Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA, Title II of the Drug Quality and Security Act, signed November 27, 2013) builds an electronic way to identify and trace prescription drugs, package by package, from manufacturer to pharmacy. It replaced the old PDMA 'pedigree' rule in 2015. It binds four trading partners: manufacturers, repackagers, wholesale distributors and dispensers (pharmacies). It is federal law, and states may not add their own tracing rules on top of it.

CoveredNot covered
Prescription drugs in finished dosage form (such as tablets, capsules, and lyophilized products before reconstitution)OTC drugs, animal drugs, blood for transfusion, radioactive and imaging drugs, certain IV fluids, medical gases, certain homeopathics, compounded drugs

The product identifier and the documents

TermWhat it is
Product identifierNDC + serial number + lot + expiration date, in a 2D data matrix barcode on each package and in human-readable text
Standardized numerical identifierThe NDC plus a unique serial number of up to 20 letters and numbers
Transaction information (TI)Product name, strength, dosage form, NDC, container size, number of containers, lot, dates, seller and buyer
Transaction statement (TS)The seller's attestation: it is authorized, bought from authorized partners, got TI and TS, did not knowingly ship suspect or illegitimate product
Transaction history (TH)The chain back to the manufacturer; no longer exchanged since November 27, 2023
Exam tip A QR code is not a DSCSA product identifier. Packages need a 2D data matrix barcode; sealed homogeneous cases may use a linear or 2D data matrix barcode.

Dates that apply now (November 2026)

DateWhat it means
Nov 27, 2023Enhanced (electronic, package-level) tracing took effect by law; FDA allowed a stabilization year to Nov 27, 2024
Nov 27, 2025FDA's exemption for dispensers with 26 or more full-time employees ended; the enhanced rules now apply to them (barring a company's own waiver)
Nov 27, 2027Small dispensers (25 or fewer full-time pharmacists and technicians, company-wide) are exempt from the enhanced rules until this date

Small dispensers still buy only from authorized partners, receive and keep TI and TS for 6 years (current methods are allowed), quarantine and investigate suspect product, and notify FDA and trading partners within 24 hours of deciding a product is illegitimate.

At receiving

  1. Check the tote or case: seal and tamper tape intact, no damage, cold-chain items still cold.
  2. Match the products, NDCs, lots and counts to the invoice and the tracing data.
  3. Look at each package: 2D barcode and printed NDC, serial, lot and expiration; label and carton look normal.
  4. Report clerical mismatches (wrong count, missing address) to the wholesaler, resolve them together within 10 business days, and document it.
  5. Anything that looks wrong goes to the marked quarantine shelf (with an electronic hold in the system where possible), never into stock; tell the pharmacist.
Safety A broken case seal or duplicate serial numbers make product suspect. A missing lot or expiration date, a mostly foreign-language label or a price 'too good to be true' are red flags: set the product aside and report it. The pharmacist decides suspect or illegitimate and releases quarantine, per the pharmacy's procedure.

Exam pearls

Exam tip Six years for DSCSA records. 24 hours to tell FDA after deciding a product is illegitimate; suspect product is quarantined, not reported. Product identifier = NDC, serial number, lot, expiration. Transaction history ended in 2023; TI and TS remain. No tracing documents when dispensing to a patient. Questions may treat November 27, 2023 as the date electronic tracing began: that is the date in the law.

Key definitions

TermMeaning under DSCSA
DispenserA retail or hospital pharmacy, a chain under common ownership that does not act as a wholesaler, or anyone authorized to dispense or administer prescription drugs, including their affiliated warehouses or distribution centers that do not act as wholesalers; not a veterinarian who uses human prescription drugs only in animals
Trading partnerA manufacturer, repackager, wholesale distributor or dispenser that takes or hands over ownership of product; a third-party logistics provider (3PL) that takes or hands over possession
AuthorizedManufacturer or repackager: valid FDA registration. Wholesaler or 3PL: valid license and annual reporting to FDA. Dispenser: valid state license
PackageThe smallest individual saleable unit the manufacturer or repackager intends to sell to a dispenser
Homogeneous caseA sealed case holding one NDC from one lot
QuarantineStoring or identifying product so it cannot be distributed, in a clearly marked separate area or by other procedures (FDA includes electronic holds)
DispositionRemoving product from the supply chain: disposal, return for disposal, or keeping a sample for testing
Specific patient needA transfer from one pharmacy to another to fill a prescription for an identified patient; not restocking

Verification means checking whether the product identifier on a package or case matches the serial number, or the lot and expiration date, that the manufacturer or repackager assigned.

Tracing documents in detail

Transaction information has ten elements: the proprietary or established name; strength and dosage form; NDC; container size; number of containers; lot number; date of the transaction; date of shipment if more than 24 hours after the transaction; and the business name and address of the seller and of the buyer. Under the enhanced requirements it must also carry the product identifier for each package, so the serial number and expiration date travel with the data.

The transaction statement is the seller's declaration that it is authorized, received the product from an authorized partner, received TI and TS from the prior owner, did not knowingly ship suspect or illegitimate product, had verification systems in place, did not knowingly give false transaction information, and did not knowingly alter the transaction history.

Transaction history (every prior transaction back to the manufacturer) stopped being required on November 27, 2023, when section 582(k) ended it. Under the enhanced requirements, each trading partner must have systems that can quickly gather the transaction information back to the manufacturer when FDA, a state official or an authorized trading partner asks during a recall or a suspect-product investigation.

Movement of productTracing documents needed?
Wholesaler sells to the pharmacyYes: the pharmacy may not accept ownership without TI and TS
Pharmacy sells stock to another pharmacyYes: the selling pharmacy provides TI and TS
Pharmacy transfers product to fill a prescription for an identified patientNo (specific patient need)
Dispensing to a patient on a prescriptionNo: dispensing is not a transaction
Minimal quantities to a licensed practitioner for office useNo: excluded from 'transaction'
Between hospitals under common control, or within one companyNo: excluded from 'transaction'
Emergency medical reasons, including a declared public health emergencyNo; a drug shortage not caused by a public health emergency is not an emergency medical reason
Sale or merger of a pharmacyNot a transaction, but the required records go to the new owner

A pharmacy may sign a written agreement for a third party, such as its wholesaler, to store its tracing documents. The pharmacy keeps a copy of the agreement and is still responsible for the records. FDA has no standard form for TI or TS. For electronic exchange FDA recommends the GS1 EPCIS standard; DSCSA does not require a Global Location Number, though a trading partner may ask for one.

When FDA or a state official asks for tracing information because of a recall or a suspect or illegitimate product, the statute gives a dispenser 2 business days (or another reasonable time FDA sets) to provide it, on paper or electronically. The enhanced requirements add that the systems to answer must work promptly.

The product identifier in detail

  • Manufacturers had to put a product identifier on each package and homogeneous case from November 2017 (FDA enforced it from November 27, 2018); repackagers from November 27, 2018. From November 27, 2020, a dispenser may handle only products that carry one.
  • Each package carries the identifier twice: machine-readable in a 2D data matrix barcode, and human-readable, which FDA recommends printing as NDC, SERIAL, LOT and EXP.
  • FDA recommends the human-readable NDC in its usual 3-segment format (labeler, product, package), not a GTIN in its place. Encoding the NDC inside a GTIN in the 2D barcode is acceptable.
  • Where space allows only a year and month, FDA reads the expiration date as the last day of that month.
  • The older linear barcode rule (21 CFR 201.25: a linear barcode carrying at least the NDC on human prescription drug labels and on OTC drugs dispensed by order and commonly used in hospitals) is separate and still applies.
  • Product packaged before November 27, 2018 without an identifier was grandfathered when there is documentation of that date; little of it should remain in date now.

Spotting suspect product

AreaFDA red flags
SourceA new or unknown supplier, an unsolicited offer, an internet seller, a partner slow or reluctant to send tracing documents, or documents with misspellings or gaps
ProductHigh demand or high price, a price 'too good to be true', a drug in shortage, a drug often counterfeited (for example HIV, antipsychotic and cancer drugs), or one under an FDA alert
Package and labelBroken, repaired or unusual seals; adhesive residue; misspellings; bubbling label; missing lot, expiration or 'Rx only'; mostly foreign language; foreign product name or foreign drug number instead of an NDC; missing holograms or other security features; lot and expiration not matching the outer case
Dosage formWrong shape, color or imprint; odd smell; chipped tablets; smeared imprints

FDA's terms: counterfeit means falsely bearing another maker's name or marks. Diverted means product that left the US supply chain (for example, after it was dispensed) and was sold back in, or product labeled for a foreign market. Stolen includes a whole package, empty packaging, or tablets removed from a bottle. Fraudulent transaction means tracing documents knowingly falsified. Unfit for distribution means nonsaleable product (for example damaged, recalled, expired or after a temperature excursion) where there is reason to believe it is likely to cause serious harm. Expired stock awaiting the reverse distributor is not treated as suspect for that reason alone.

Product a patient brings back with a complaint is in the pharmacy's possession, so the pharmacy looks into whether it is suspect. Even if it is cleared, FDA says it should not be dispensed or distributed again.

Investigating suspect product

  1. Quarantine the product away from saleable stock, physically and, where possible, with an electronic hold, until it is cleared or dispositioned.
  2. Validate the TI (and any transaction history) the pharmacy holds: do the NDC and lot in the records match the product?
  3. Verify the lot number: from the TI, or with the manufacturer or repackager if the TI does not show it.
  4. Verify the product identifier, including the serial number, with the manufacturer or repackager for at least 3 packages or 10% of the suspect product, whichever is more (all packages if fewer than 3). For example, 20 packages: check 3; 50 packages: check 5.
  5. Coordinate with the wholesaler and the manufacturer, which usually does any laboratory testing; the pharmacy may rely on the manufacturer's findings.
  6. Record how the decision was reached and keep the investigation record 6 years after it ends.

If the product is cleared, it may be dispensed. The pharmacy sends FDA a 'Cleared Product Notification' only when FDA had asked it to verify that product; Form FDA 3911 is not used for this.

Safety Only the person the pharmacy's procedure names (FDA gives the pharmacist-in-charge as an example) may release product from quarantine. A technician never moves quarantined stock back to the shelf.

Illegitimate product and Form FDA 3911

  • The pharmacy decides, in coordination with the manufacturer, that the product is illegitimate.
  • Within 24 hours of that decision, it notifies FDA and every immediate trading partner it believes may have received the product (those it sold the product to and, in some cases, the supplier it bought from).
  • FDA prefers the 3911 platform in its CDER NextGen portal; the alternative is the fillable Form FDA 3911 sent by email to drugnotifications@fda.hhs.gov. The form covers who is reporting, the product, and what happened, and classifies the problem as counterfeit, diverted, stolen, intentional adulteration, unfit for distribution or fraudulent transaction.
  • FDA replies with an incident number, used on every follow-up and on the request to end the notification.
  • The pharmacy keeps the product quarantined, dispositions it, keeps a sample if the manufacturer or FDA asks, and helps trading partners remove product it no longer holds.
  • When a notification is no longer needed, the pharmacy asks FDA to end it (a consultation), then tells the partners it notified.
  • Records of the disposition are kept 6 years after it ends.
  • When the pharmacy receives a notice that a product is illegitimate, it finds all of that product in its possession, including later deliveries, and quarantines and investigates it.
Safety A stolen controlled substance is also a DEA matter: the registrant notifies the DEA Field Division in writing within one business day of discovery and files DEA Form 106. DSCSA notification to FDA does not replace it.

Enhanced drug distribution security (section 582(g))

From November 27, 2023, the law requires interoperable, electronic tracing at the package level. In practice the requirements are:

  • TI and TS exchanged securely and electronically, to FDA's data standards.
  • TI that includes the product identifier of every package.
  • Systems to verify product at the package level, which may use aggregation and inference.
  • Systems to answer FDA or a state promptly with TI and TS during a recall or investigation.
  • Systems to gather TI back to the manufacturer when FDA, a state or an authorized trading partner asks.
  • Saleable returns accepted only when the returned product can be matched to its TI and TS.

Aggregation links the serial numbers of packages to the serial number of the case or pallet they ship in. Inference means trusting that link instead of scanning every package. FDA says to infer only when the manufacturer's or repackager's seal on the case or pallet is intact. A broken or altered seal that the buyer did not open makes the contents suspect.

Clerical errors happen: 9 bottles received when the data list 10, extra bottles, an item listed twice, a missing case identifier, a missing or wrong buyer address. FDA asks the buyer to tell the seller, resolve the error together within 10 business days, and document the error, the fix, the people involved and the date. Two packages with the same serial number are not a clerical error: treat them as suspect.

Stabilization, exemptions and small dispensers

WhoRelief grantedStatus on 2026-11-15
All trading partnersFDA compliance policies (August 2023): a stabilization period from Nov 27, 2023 to Nov 27, 2024Ended
Manufacturers and repackagers that had started their data connectionsFDA exemption (October 9, 2024) to May 27, 2025Ended
Wholesale distributors that had started their data connectionsFDA exemption to August 27, 2025Ended
Dispensers with 26 or more full-time employees that had started their data connectionsFDA exemption to November 27, 2025Ended; enhanced rules apply unless the company holds its own FDA waiver or exemption
Small business dispensersFDA exemption (July 12, 2024) to Nov 27, 2026, extended (August 6, 2026) to November 27, 2027In force

For the exemption, a pharmacy is a small business dispenser if the company that owns it has, in total, 25 or fewer full-time employees licensed as pharmacists or qualified as pharmacy technicians, counted as of November 27, 2026. Full-time follows the IRS definition: on average at least 30 hours a week, or 130 hours a month. The statute itself speaks of dispensers with '25 or fewer full-time employees' without limiting the count to pharmacists and technicians; FDA's definition admits more pharmacies. A small dispenser that uses the exemption sends nothing to FDA, though FDA recommends telling its trading partners.

The extension gives FDA time to finish the small dispenser assessment that section 582(g)(3) requires: an independent study of whether the software and hardware for package-level tracing are available, affordable and workable for small pharmacies, followed by public comment and a public meeting. Trading partners that do not qualify for an exemption may ask FDA for their own waiver, exception or exemption through CDER NextGen, and are expected to keep working toward compliance while the request is pending.

Federal and state law

For most pharmacy law, a state may be stricter than federal law. Product tracing is an exception. Section 585 of the FD&C Act bars any state from tracing, verification, investigation, notification or recordkeeping requirements for drug tracing that are inconsistent with, more stringent than or in addition to DSCSA, including state pedigree systems. States still license pharmacies, wholesalers and 3PLs (a pharmacy is 'authorized' because it holds a valid state license), can enforce, and can suspend or revoke licenses.

Who does what in the pharmacy

TaskUsually
Inspect totes and seals, match the shipment to the invoice and tracing data, check identifiersTechnician
Set questionable product aside in quarantine, label it, record it, tell the pharmacistTechnician
Send clerical errors back to the wholesaler and file the corrected documentsTechnician or buyer, per procedure
Decide whether product is suspect or illegitimate, coordinate with the manufacturerPharmacist (often the pharmacist-in-charge)
Notify FDA (3911), notify trading partners, request terminationPharmacist or pharmacy management
Release product from quarantineOnly the person the procedure authorizes

The law puts every duty on 'the dispenser', meaning the pharmacy as a business. Each pharmacy's written procedure says who does each step. A technician who finds a problem stops the product from reaching a patient and reports it; the technician does not decide that a product is safe.
