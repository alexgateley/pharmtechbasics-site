Exam tip Dispensing Process is 43 of the 100 scored items, more than any two other domains together. Intake, billing, dispensing, math and compounding all sit here.

How it differs from the PTCE

  • Still tested on the ExCPT, dropped from the 2026 PTCE: non-sterile compounding and alligation.
  • On the ExCPT test plan, not listed in the PTCE outline: sterile compounding and aseptic technique, business math (markup, gross and net profit), third-party billing (BIN, PCN, rejections, prior authorization, coordination of benefits), DAW codes, general inventory management such as PAR levels (the PTCE names ordering only for controlled substances), and pediatric dose rules (Young's, Clark's, Fried's).
  • Longer: 120 items in 2 hours 10 minutes, against 90 items in a 2-hour PTCE appointment.
  • Different scales: 390 on 200 to 500 (ExCPT) versus 1,400 on 1,000 to 1,600 (PTCE).
  • The ExCPT can be taken at home with a live remote proctor; PTCB's CPhT page lists only in-person Pearson test centers.
Exam tip A book or practice test written only for the 2026 PTCE may leave out compounding, alligation and billing. For the ExCPT, study them.

How to study: 6 weeks

  1. Week 1: Read the free test plan. Set your exam date in Settings. Take a timed Mock Exam (120 items, 130 minutes) as a baseline. Then Domain 1 with the Storage, Recalls, Returns and Disposal page.
  2. Week 2: Drugs and Drug Therapy: the drug cards by class and Pharmacology by Class and Suffix. Daily Review every day from here on.
  3. Week 3: Laws: HIPAA in the Pharmacy, Poison Prevention Packaging Act, OBRA 90, DEA Schedules, Forms and Controlled Prescriptions, then the CMEA and DSCSA pages.
  4. Week 4: Intake and dispensing: Prescription Intake and Processing, Sig Codes and Pharmacy Abbreviations, and Recalls, REMS, Medication Guides and Reporting. Learn BIN, PCN, DAW codes and rejections.
  5. Week 5: Calculations and compounding: Pharmacy Math with the Days Supply, Dilution, Alligation, Percent and Ratio Strength, Milligrams and Milliequivalents, and Markup and Gross Profit calculators; Compounding and Beyond-Use Dates; Hazardous Drugs.
  6. Week 6: Medication Safety and Error Prevention. A second Mock Exam, then Weakest Subject, Missed Questions and Trouble Questions. Rest the day before.
Exam tip If all 130 minutes are for items, you get about 65 seconds each. The app Mock Exam uses the full sitting: 120 items in 130 minutes.

Test-day tips

  • Bring a current government-issued photo ID with your signature and permanent address. The name must match your registration exactly. If the address differs, bring proof such as a current utility bill.
  • Use the calculator built into the exam. Personal calculators and your computer's calculator are not allowed.
  • PSI centers give you a whiteboard and marker; remote exams have a digital whiteboard.
  • Remote: run PSI's system check first. No breaks, no leaving the room, no one else in the room.
  • In person: time out of the room still counts against your clock. No food or drink without an approved accommodation.
  • You cannot tell pretest items from scored ones. Give every item full effort, and answer every item: a blank cannot earn credit.
  • Need to move the date? With PSI (center or remote), reschedule at least 24 hours ahead, or the fee is lost. At a school or employer site, ask the site before the exam starts.

Technician or pharmacist?

Domain 1 starts with telling apart what a technician may do and what the pharmacist must do. The test plan's own tasks show the line: the technician assists, prepares and refers; the pharmacist oversees, verifies and counsels.

The technicianThe pharmacist
Makes sure final verification is done before a prescription goes to the patientDoes the final verification
Offers the patient a consultation with the pharmacistCounsels the patient
Gets the OTC product or supplement the pharmacist recommendsMakes the OTC recommendation
Gathers the medication list for reconciliationReviews and reconciles it
Helps with MTM follow-up calls and spots signs of poor adherenceLeads the MTM review and any therapy change
Responds to electronic alerts during entry (for example, a third-party rejection) and brings clinical DUR alerts such as interactions to the pharmacistReviews clinical alerts and decides what to do
Exam tip When an answer has the technician counseling, recommending a product or changing therapy, it is usually wrong. The best answer refers the patient to the pharmacist.

Law items test federal rules. Your state board of pharmacy may be stricter, and NHA certification is separate from any state registration or licensure.

Eligibility and applying

  • Step 1: a high school diploma or the equivalent (such as a GED). If you will graduate within 12 months, you can test and receive a provisional certification, which you must convert within 12 months of the exam date.
  • Step 2, Pathway 1: within the past 5 years, complete a pharmacy technician training program from an accredited or state-approved provider, a registered apprenticeship, military pharmacy technician training, or an employer-based or national pharmacy association program recognized by your state board or verified by your employer.
  • Some schools let you test after the coursework and before the externship. You then have 90 days from the exam date to finish the externship and have the school notify NHA, or the score is voided.
  • Step 2, Pathway 2: at least 1,200 hours of supervised pharmacy-related work during a 1-year period within the last 3 years.
  • Apply yourself through your NHA account (no one else may register for you) and agree to the ExCPT attestation. Unless a school or employer pays, pay within 2 hours of applying, or the application is voided.
  • You have 6 months from your application date to schedule and take the exam with PSI.
  • Need accommodations? Send NHA's request form with documentation and allow 30 days. Approved extra time is 150% of the standard time. Remote testing may not be available with some accommodations. The exam is given only in English.

Results, retakes and renewal

  • Scoring: each form is scaled for difficulty so 390 means the same on every form. Pretest items do not count.
  • Score report: in your NHA account within 48 hours for PSI center and remote exams; a preliminary report at once if you test at your school or employer (preliminary is not final). It shows pass or fail, your score, and Above, Near or Below the passing standard for each domain.
  • If you do not pass: study every domain marked Near or Below; Near is not a safe result. Do not skip Above domains, since each category is a range.
  • Retakes: wait at least 30 days between attempts. After a third failed attempt, wait 1 year before each further attempt. Each retake means registering again and paying the full fee.
  • Rescoring: ask in writing within 60 days of the exam; NHA may charge a fee. Score appeals: NHA's appeals form within 30 days.
  • Refunds: only if NHA, PSI or the remote proctoring provider cancels and you do not reschedule.
  • Renewal: every 2 years, with 20 CE hours that include at least 1 hour of pharmacy law and 1 hour of patient safety, plus the recertification fee. An expired certification can be reinstated within 1 year; after that you must retake the exam.
