Question 1

In a hospital receiving area, a technician scans the 2D data matrix bar code on a carton of ondansetron 2 mg/mL injection vials. Under the DSCSA, which element of the product identifier tells this carton apart from other cartons of the same NDC and lot?

  1. The expiration date
  2. The serial number
  3. The package code of the NDC
  4. The lot number

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Every carton made in a lot carries the same expiration date, so it does not single out one package.
  • B. Correct. The standardized numerical identifier joins the NDC with a serial number of up to 20 characters that is unique to each package.
  • C. The package code marks the size and type of package, so every carton of this configuration carries the same one.
  • D. A lot covers a whole manufactured batch, so many cartons share it, and the stem already holds the lot constant.

Under the Drug Supply Chain Security Act, the product identifier is a standardized graphic, in human-readable and machine-readable form, that carries the standardized numerical identifier, the lot number and the expiration date. The standardized numerical identifier is the NDC for that package configuration combined with a unique alphanumeric serial number. Because the serial number differs on each package, it lets trading partners verify one package, trace it, and quarantine suspect product down to a single carton rather than a whole lot.

Sources: FDA: Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA)

Question 2

A technician receives a prescription for clopidogrel 75 mg tablets. Sig: take 4 tablets (300 mg) by mouth on day 1, then 1 tablet daily starting on day 2. The prescriber asks for a 30-day supply, counting day 1 as the first day. How many tablets should be dispensed?

  1. 33 tablets
  2. 31 tablets
  3. 34 tablets
  4. 120 tablets

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Day 1 uses 4 tablets, and days 2 through 30 are 29 more days at 1 tablet each, so 4 + 29 = 33 tablets.
  • B. Tempting if the loading dose is counted as one tablet (1 + 30), but 300 mg from 75 mg tablets takes 4 tablets on day 1.
  • C. Tempting because it adds the 4-tablet loading dose to 30 daily doses (4 + 30), but that covers 31 days, since the first daily tablet is not taken until day 2.
  • D. Tempting if the loading dose is read as the daily dose (4 × 30), but 4 tablets is a one-time load; the maintenance dose is 75 mg once daily.

A loading dose changes the count only on the day it is given, so work day 1 separately from the maintenance days. Day 1: 300 ÷ 75 = 4 tablets. Days 2 to 30: 29 days at 1 tablet, 29 × 1 = 29 tablets. Total: 4 + 29 = 33 tablets for 30 days. The Plavix label directs a single 300 mg oral loading dose for acute coronary syndrome and then 75 mg once daily, and notes that starting without the load delays the antiplatelet effect by several days, which is why the sig front-loads day 1. Brand Plavix also comes as a 300 mg tablet, but this prescription is written for the 75 mg strength, so the label and count follow the 75 mg tablet.

Sources: PLAVIX (clopidogrel) Prescribing Information

Question 3

On the day of its biennial controlled substance inventory, a hospital pharmacy has a sealed tote in the vault holding expired dextroamphetamine sulfate 10 mg tablets (Schedule II) and expired eszopiclone 2 mg tablets (Schedule IV) awaiting next week's reverse distributor pickup. Under federal law, how should the technician helping with the inventory handle the tote?

  1. Include both drugs, noting they are held for disposal
  2. List both drugs on a DEA Form 41 instead of the inventory
  3. Leave both drugs out until the reverse distributor picks them up
  4. Count the dextroamphetamine and leave out the eszopiclone

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: A

  • A. Correct. Controlled substances awaiting disposal are still stock on hand, so both go on the inventory with the quantity and the reason they are held.
  • B. Tempting because Form 41 is a disposal form, but it records destruction, which the reverse distributor documents; it does not replace the inventory.
  • C. Tempting because the drugs can no longer be dispensed, but the inventory must list every controlled substance in the pharmacy's possession, including expired stock.
  • D. Tempting because Schedule II drugs carry the strictest counting rules, but Schedule III-V drugs are inventoried too; only the counting method differs for opened containers.

An inventory is a complete list of every controlled substance the registrant possesses on the inventory date, taken at least every two years and kept at the registered location for two years. Under 21 CFR 1304.11, damaged, defective or impure drugs awaiting disposal are listed with the drug name, the quantity or number of units, and the reason the pharmacy is holding them. Schedule II drugs need an exact count; opened Schedule III-V containers of 1,000 units or fewer may be estimated. The inventory notes whether it was taken at opening or close of business, and the Schedule II records are kept separate.

Sources: 21 CFR Part 1304 (controlled substance records and inventories); DEA Pharmacist's Manual (2022)

Question 4

An IV room technician is checking four vancomycin intermittent infusions against the label's recommendation that diluted vancomycin not exceed 5 mg/mL in adults. None of the patients is on a fluid restriction. Which preparation should the technician hold for the pharmacist because it exceeds that concentration?

  1. 1.75 g in 500 mL
  2. 1 g in 200 mL
  3. 1.5 g in 250 mL
  4. 1.25 g in 250 mL

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Tempting because it is the largest dose, but 1,750 mg ÷ 500 mL = 3.5 mg/mL, well under the limit.
  • B. Tempting because 1 g in a small bag looks concentrated, but 1,000 mg ÷ 200 mL = 5 mg/mL, which meets the limit of no more than 5 mg/mL.
  • C. Correct. 1,500 mg ÷ 250 mL = 6 mg/mL, above the 5 mg/mL recommended for adults; 1.5 g needs at least 300 mL.
  • D. Tempting because it sits exactly at 5 mg/mL (1,250 mg ÷ 250 mL), but a limit of no more than 5 mg/mL includes 5 mg/mL itself.

Convert grams to milligrams before dividing, because the limit is stated in mg/mL: 1.5 × 1,000 = 1,500 mg, and 1,500 ÷ 250 = 6 mg/mL. The vancomycin label recommends diluted concentrations of no more than 5 mg/mL in adults, infused over 60 minutes or longer, and allows up to 10 mg/mL only in selected patients who need fluid restriction, at a higher risk of infusion-related reactions. The smallest bag that keeps 1,500 mg at 5 mg/mL is 1,500 ÷ 5 = 300 mL. Choosing a new diluent volume or a fluid-restriction exception is the pharmacist's call, so the technician holds the bag rather than changing it.

Sources: Vancomycin for injection label (Hikma, 2024)

Question 5

A long-term care resident who can no longer swallow tablets has an order for digoxin 0.125 mg by mouth once daily. The pharmacy supplies digoxin oral solution 50 mcg/mL. What volume should the label give for each dose?

  1. 0.4 mL
  2. 0.25 mL
  3. 6.25 mL
  4. 2.5 mL

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: D

  • A. This inverts the ratio (50 ÷ 125), dividing the concentration by the dose rather than the dose by the concentration.
  • B. This is a tenfold decimal slip, the kind that happens when 50 mcg/mL is read as 0.5 mg/mL instead of 0.05 mg/mL.
  • C. This multiplies 125 by 0.05, the concentration written in mg/mL, instead of dividing the dose by the concentration in matching units.
  • D. Correct. 0.125 mg is 125 mcg, and 125 mcg ÷ 50 mcg/mL = 2.5 mL.

Put the dose and the concentration in the same unit first: 0.125 mg × 1,000 = 125 mcg. Then divide the dose by the concentration: 125 mcg ÷ 50 mcg/mL = 2.5 mL. Digoxin has a narrow therapeutic range, so a tenfold slip can cause toxicity. Stating the dose on the label in both mcg and mL, and measuring it with an oral syringe rather than a household spoon, helps the facility give exactly 125 mcg.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 6

A technician is entering a prescription for butalbital, acetaminophen and caffeine 50 mg/325 mg/40 mg tablets, quantity 45. Sig: take 1 to 2 tablets by mouth every 6 hours as needed for headache; do not exceed 6 tablets in 24 hours. What days supply should be entered?

  1. 22 days
  2. 5 days
  3. 7 days
  4. 11 days

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Tempting because 45 tablets at 2 per dose gives about 22 doses, but that counts doses, not days, and the patient may take up to four doses a day.
  • B. Tempting because it uses the high end of the dose, 2 tablets every 6 hours (8 a day, 45 ÷ 8 = 5.6), but the sig caps the day at 6 tablets, so 8 a day is not allowed.
  • C. Correct. A range or as-needed sig is entered at the most the patient may take, which here is the 6-tablet daily cap: 45 ÷ 6 = 7.5, entered as 7 days.
  • D. Tempting because 1 tablet every 6 hours is the low end of the range (4 a day, 45 ÷ 4 = 11.25), but entering the smallest dose overstates how long the supply can last and lets an early refill slip past the plan's edits.

Days supply for an as-needed or range sig is calculated from the maximum the prescriber allows, because that is how quickly the patient could use the quantity and it is what refill-too-soon edits expect. Here the stated cap of 6 tablets in 24 hours is lower than the 8 tablets a day that 2 tablets every 6 hours would permit, so the cap governs: 45 ÷ 6 = 7.5, and the half day is dropped, giving 7 days. The generic tablet label sets the same ceiling (1 or 2 tablets every 4 hours, total daily dosage not to exceed 6 tablets) and warns that butalbital is habit-forming, so an accurate days supply matters for refill timing.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 7

A mail-order pharmacy technician processes a refill for Victoza (liraglutide) injection 18 mg/3 mL pens. The prescriber has increased the dose from 1.2 mg to 1.8 mg subcutaneously once daily, and the patient's last 90-day fill was 6 pens. Victoza comes in cartons of 2 or 3 pens. What quantity covers a 90-day supply at the new dose?

  1. 6 pens, as on the last fill
  2. 3 pens, one for each 30 days of use
  3. 9 pens in three 3-pen cartons
  4. 27 pens in nine 3-pen cartons

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Six pens covered 90 days at 1.2 mg (15 doses per pen), but at 1.8 mg each pen gives 10 doses, so 6 pens last 60 days.
  • B. The 30-day rule limits how long an opened pen may be used, not how long it lasts; at 1.8 mg a pen is empty after 10 days.
  • C. Correct. 1.8 mg × 90 days = 162 mg, and 162 mg ÷ 18 mg per pen = 9 pens, which three 3-pen cartons supply exactly.
  • D. This divides 162 mg by the 6 mg/mL concentration, which gives 27 mL of drug, not 27 pens; at 3 mL per pen, 27 mL is 9 pens.

Each Victoza pen holds 18 mg in 3 mL (6 mg/mL) and delivers doses of 0.6, 1.2 or 1.8 mg. At 1.8 mg a day, one pen gives 18 ÷ 1.8 = 10 doses, so 90 days needs 90 ÷ 10 = 9 pens. The label allows an opened pen to be kept for 30 days at room temperature or in the refrigerator, and each pen here is finished in 10 days, so the in-use limit does not raise the count. Unopened pens are refrigerated at 2 to 8 °C and must not be frozen, which matters for mail-order packing. When a dose changes, the quantity on the profile must be recalculated, not copied from the last fill.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 8

A caregiver picking up budesonide inhalation suspension 0.5 mg/2 mL (Pulmicort Respules) also brings a prescription for a home nebulizer, and the technician pulls the device from the pharmacy's medical equipment stock. Which type of device matches the product's labeling?

  1. Pulmicort Flexhaler device
  2. Compressor-driven jet nebulizer
  3. Portable ultrasonic nebulizer with mask
  4. Valved holding chamber with face mask

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Tempting because it carries the same brand name, but the Flexhaler is a separate dry powder budesonide product, not a device for Respules ampules.
  • B. Correct. The label directs giving the suspension by a jet nebulizer connected to an air compressor, with a mouthpiece or suitable face mask.
  • C. Tempting because ultrasonic units are small and quiet, but the label says ultrasonic nebulizers are not suitable for budesonide suspension and are not recommended.
  • D. Tempting because it is a common inhaler accessory, but a holding chamber is used with a metered-dose inhaler and cannot aerosolize a liquid suspension.

Pulmicort Respules are single-dose 2 mL ampules of budesonide suspension made for compressed-air jet nebulization; the label states ultrasonic nebulizers do not deliver the suspension adequately. It also says not to mix the suspension with other nebulized drugs without data, to store ampules upright at controlled room temperature protected from light, and to use the unused ampules within 2 weeks after the foil envelope is opened. Which device suits a given patient beyond the label is a question for the pharmacist.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed)

Question 9

In a hospital IV room, a technician receives an order for cefazolin 1 g IVPB q8h for a surgical patient. Which preparation matches the route written in the order?

  1. The dose in a syringe for slow injection over 3 to 5 minutes
  2. The dose added to a 1,000 mL bag to run continuously
  3. The dose diluted in a 50 mL bag for intermittent infusion
  4. The dose in a syringe for deep injection into a large muscle

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: C

  • A. Tempting because the cefazolin label allows direct injection over 3 to 5 minutes, but that route is IV push (IVP), not IV piggyback.
  • B. Tempting because a large-volume bag is also given intravenously, but a continuous infusion is not intermittent dosing every 8 hours; IVPB is a small-volume bag.
  • C. Correct. IVPB means intravenous piggyback: the dose goes in a small-volume bag that is infused intermittently through the patient's primary IV line.
  • D. Tempting because cefazolin can be given intramuscularly, but that route would be written IM; IVPB names an intravenous route.

IVPB (intravenous piggyback) is an intermittent infusion: a small-volume bag, commonly 50 to 100 mL, hung as a secondary line that runs into the primary IV line over a set time. The cefazolin label describes three ways to give the drug: intramuscular injection, direct IV injection slowly over 3 to 5 minutes, and intermittent or continuous infusion after the reconstituted vial is diluted in 50 to 100 mL of a compatible fluid such as sodium chloride 0.9% or dextrose 5%. The q8h frequency means one bag every 8 hours, three doses in 24 hours, so the IV room labels each bag with its own administration time.

Sources: Cefazolin for injection label (Hikma, 2026)

Question 10

A technician at a long-term care pharmacy is entering four new prescriptions before they go to the pharmacist for verification. Which prescription is a 30-day supply?

  1. Baclofen 10 mg, 1 tablet three times daily, #84
  2. Clonidine 0.1 mg, 1 tablet twice daily, #60
  3. Bupropion SR 150 mg, 1 tablet twice daily, #30
  4. Carvedilol 6.25 mg, 1 tablet twice daily, #90

Show the answer and rationale

Answer: B

  • A. Tempting because 84 tablets looks like a month of three-times-daily dosing, but 84 ÷ 3 = 28 days, a four-week supply rather than 30 days.
  • B. Correct. Two tablets a day from 60 tablets: 60 ÷ 2 = 30 days.
  • C. Tempting because #30 usually signals a month, but twice-daily dosing uses 30 tablets in 30 ÷ 2 = 15 days.
  • D. Tempting because carvedilol is often filled in 90s, but at two tablets a day 90 ÷ 2 = 45 days; 90 tablets would be 30 days at three a day.

Days supply is the quantity divided by the units used per day, so each prescription has to be worked out rather than judged by a familiar quantity such as #30 or #90. Clonidine 0.1 mg is a labeled starting dose taken twice daily, morning and bedtime, so 60 tablets last 30 days. Baclofen 10 mg three times daily is 3 tablets a day, which makes 84 tablets a 28-day fill. Carvedilol twice daily with food makes 90 tablets last 45 days, and the usual Wellbutrin SR target of 150 mg twice daily uses 30 tablets in 15 days. A wrong figure leads to claim rejections or refill-too-soon edits, or to a patient running out early.

Sources: FDA Prescribing Information (DailyMed); Wellbutrin SR (bupropion) label (GSK, 2025)
