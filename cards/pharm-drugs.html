ACE inhibitors

High blood pressure, heart failure, after a heart attack

Common drugs

LisinoprilZestril · tablets; Qbrelis · oral solution
EnalaprilVasotec · tablets; Epaned · oral solution
BenazeprilLotensin · tablets; Lotensin HCT (with hydrochlorothiazide)
RamiprilGeneric only · capsules (Altace no longer marketed)
QuinaprilGeneric only · tablets (Accupril no longer marketed)
CaptoprilGeneric only · tablets

What to know

  • Stem: -pril.
  • Used for: hypertension, heart failure, and after an acute heart attack (Zestril label).
  • Side effects: dry cough; angioedema (swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat) is rare and an emergency; high potassium.
  • Handling: boxed warning for fetal toxicity (stop when pregnancy is found). Epaned is stored refrigerated in the pharmacy; the patient may keep it at room temperature for up to 60 days. Qbrelis is stored at room temperature.
  • Names ending in HCT add hydrochlorothiazide: a second drug, a second duplication check.
  • Look-alike: lisinopril and lisinopril/hydrochlorothiazide sit side by side on the shelf; check for the second ingredient.
Safety An ACE inhibitor and sacubitril/valsartan (Entresto) must never overlap: the label requires a 36-hour gap when switching. Route any profile with both to the pharmacist.

Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs)

High blood pressure; some also heart failure or diabetic kidney disease

Common drugs

LosartanCozaar · tablets; Hyzaar (with hydrochlorothiazide)
ValsartanDiovan · tablets; Diovan HCT
OlmesartanBenicar · tablets; Benicar HCT
IrbesartanAvapro · tablets
TelmisartanGeneric only for the single drug; Micardis HCT · tablets
CandesartanAtacand · tablets; Atacand HCT
AzilsartanEdarbi · tablets

What to know

  • Stem: -sartan.
  • Used for: hypertension (all); heart failure (valsartan, candesartan); diabetic nephropathy (losartan, irbesartan).
  • Side effects: dizziness, high potassium; less cough than ACE inhibitors.
  • Handling: boxed warning for fetal toxicity, as with ACE inhibitors.
  • Duplication: two ARBs, or an ARB with an ACE inhibitor, on one profile is a flag for the pharmacist. Entresto already contains valsartan.
  • Look-alike names: Cozaar (losartan) and Zocor (simvastatin).

High blood pressure, angina, heart failure, after a heart attack

AtenololTenormin · tablets

BisoprololGeneric only · tablets (Zebeta no longer marketed)
LabetalolGeneric only · tablets, injection

What to know

  • Stem: -olol (carvedilol and labetalol end in -ilol and -alol).
  • Used for: hypertension, angina, heart failure (metoprolol succinate, carvedilol), after a heart attack.
  • Side effects: slow heart rate, dizziness, tiredness; can hide the warning signs of low blood sugar.
  • Do not stop suddenly: abrupt stopping can worsen angina (label warning).
  • Tartrate and succinate are different products: Lopressor is immediate release, Toprol-XL is extended release. Fill the salt and release form written.
  • Handling: Toprol-XL is scored and may be split, but not crushed or chewed. Coreg CR is swallowed whole with food; not crushed, chewed or divided.
  • Look-alike names: Toprol-XL (metoprolol) and Topamax (topiramate).

Calcium channel blockers

High blood pressure and angina; diltiazem and verapamil also slow the heart rate

Common drugs

AmlodipineNorvasc · tablets; Katerzia · oral suspension; Norliqva · oral solution
Nifedipine ERProcardia XL · extended-release tablets
Felodipine ERGeneric only · extended-release tablets
DiltiazemCardizem · tablets; Cardizem CD, Tiazac · ER capsules; Cardizem LA · ER tablets
VerapamilGeneric only · IR and ER tablets, ER capsules (Calan SR no longer marketed)

What to know

  • Stem: -dipine marks the dihydropyridines (amlodipine, nifedipine, felodipine). Diltiazem and verapamil are nondihydropyridines and also slow the heart.
  • Used for: hypertension and angina.
  • Side effects: ankle swelling (the most common effect of amlodipine), flushing, dizziness; slow heart rate with diltiazem and verapamil.
  • Handling: Procardia XL and Cardizem LA are swallowed whole. Tiazac capsules may be opened and sprinkled on applesauce (label).
  • Diltiazem ER brands use different release systems: fill the exact product written. Substitution follows the Orange Book ratings, and the pharmacist decides.
  • Storage: Katerzia suspension is refrigerated and protected from light; Norliqva solution is kept at room temperature.
  • Look-alike names: NIFEdipine and niCARdipine (an FDA tall man lettering pair); Norvasc (amlodipine) and Navane (thiothixene).

Thiazide and loop diuretics

High blood pressure (thiazides) and fluid overload or edema (loops)

Common drugs

HydrochlorothiazideGeneric tablets, capsules; Inzirqo · oral suspension
ChlorthalidoneThalitone · tablets; generic tablets
MetolazoneGeneric only · tablets
IndapamideGeneric only · tablets
FurosemideLasix · tablets; Furoscix · subcutaneous on-body infusor or ReadyFlow autoinjector
BumetanideBumex · tablets
TorsemideSoaanz · tablets; generic tablets

What to know

  • Stems: -thiazide for the thiazides; chlorthalidone, metolazone and indapamide are thiazide-like. Loops: furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide.
  • Used for: hypertension (thiazides); edema from heart failure, liver or kidney disease (loops).
  • Side effects: more urination, dehydration, low potassium, dizziness.
  • Lasix and Bumex labels carry a boxed warning: a potent diuretic can cause profound loss of water and electrolytes.
  • Handling: Lasix tablets go in light-resistant containers, and discolored tablets must not be dispensed (label).
  • Abbreviations: HCTZ (and HCT) are error-prone drug-name abbreviations (ISMP concept); enter the full drug name.

Potassium-sparing diuretics and MRAs

Heart failure, hypertension and edema; finerenone for kidney disease with type 2 diabetes and for heart failure

Common drugs

SpironolactoneAldactone · tablets; CaroSpir · oral suspension
EplerenoneInspra · tablets
Triamterene/hydrochlorothiazideGeneric only · capsules, tablets (Dyazide, Maxzide not listed)
AmilorideGeneric only · tablets
FinerenoneKerendia · tablets

What to know

  • No shared stem. Spironolactone and eplerenone block aldosterone (MRAs); finerenone is a newer MRA.
  • Used for: heart failure, hypertension, edema; Kerendia for chronic kidney disease with type 2 diabetes and for heart failure with LVEF 40% or more (label 9/2026).
  • Side effects: high potassium (boxed warning on amiloride and on triamterene/hydrochlorothiazide); breast enlargement with spironolactone.
  • Flag for the pharmacist: potassium supplements, salt substitutes, or an ACE inhibitor or ARB on the same profile.
  • CaroSpir is not therapeutically equivalent to Aldactone tablets (label): it is not a mg-for-mg swap. Shake well.

HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors (statins)

Lower LDL cholesterol and the risk of heart attack and stroke

Common drugs

AtorvastatinLipitor · tablets; Atorvaliq · oral suspension
SimvastatinZocor · tablets
RosuvastatinCrestor · tablets
PravastatinGeneric only · tablets (Pravachol no longer marketed)
LovastatinGeneric only · tablets (Altoprev no longer marketed)
PitavastatinLivalo (calcium) · tablets; Zypitamag (magnesium) · tablets
FluvastatinLescol XL · ER tablets

What to know

  • Stem: -statin.
  • Side effects: muscle pain; rarely muscle breakdown (myopathy, rhabdomyolysis).
  • Timing: Zocor is taken in the evening; Crestor at any time of day (labels).
  • Zocor is no longer marketed in 5 mg and 80 mg, and the 80 mg dose is restricted (label).
  • Interaction flag: simvastatin with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, such as clarithromycin, is contraindicated. Route it to the pharmacist.
  • Handling: Atorvaliq is dispensed in its original bottle and used within 60 days of opening.
  • Not a statin: ezetimibe (Zetia) lowers cholesterol another way and is often added.

Anticoagulants: warfarin and DOACs

Prevent stroke in atrial fibrillation; treat and prevent blood clots

Common drugs

WarfarinJantoven (branded generic) · tablets, NDC listings end 12/2026; generic tablets (Coumadin no longer marketed)
ApixabanEliquis · tablets, tablets for oral suspension; Eliquis Sprinkle · capsules for oral suspension
RivaroxabanXarelto · tablets
DabigatranPradaxa · capsules, oral pellets
EdoxabanSavaysa · tablets
EnoxaparinLovenox · prefilled syringes, multiple-dose vial

What to know

  • Stems: -xaban (factor Xa inhibitors), -gatran (direct thrombin inhibitor), -parin (heparins). Warfarin is a vitamin K antagonist monitored by INR.
  • Side effect: bleeding (boxed warning on warfarin). DOAC labels add a boxed warning that stopping early raises clot risk.
  • A Medication Guide goes with every warfarin, Eliquis, Xarelto, Pradaxa and Savaysa fill.
  • Warfarin tablets are color-coded by strength (1 mg pink through 10 mg white); protect from light.
  • Pradaxa capsules stay in the original bottle, are used within 4 months of opening, and are never opened, broken or chewed.
  • Eliquis tablets may be crushed and mixed with water, apple juice or applesauce (label). Xarelto 15 and 20 mg are taken with food.
  • Anticoagulants are high-alert medications (ISMP concept): errors cause serious harm. New interacting drugs (antibiotics, NSAIDs, antiplatelets) go to the pharmacist.

Antiplatelets

Prevent heart attack and stroke, and clots in coronary stents

Common drugs

Aspirin (low dose)Bayer, Ecotrin (OTC) · chewable and coated (enteric) tablets
ClopidogrelPlavix · tablets
PrasugrelEffient · tablets
TicagrelorBrilinta · tablets
CilostazolGeneric only · tablets (Pletal no longer marketed)

What to know

  • Stem: -grel (P2Y12 inhibitors: clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor).
  • Side effect: bleeding (boxed warning on Effient and Brilinta).
  • Plavix boxed warning: less effect in people who are CYP2C19 poor metabolizers.
  • Cilostazol boxed warning: contraindicated in heart failure of any severity.
  • A Medication Guide goes with Plavix, Effient and Brilinta.
  • Flag for the pharmacist: an antiplatelet with an anticoagulant or a regular NSAID (bleeding risk).
  • Look-alike names: Plavix (clopidogrel) and Paxil (paroxetine).

Nitrates and other antianginals

Relieve or prevent angina (chest pain)

Common drugs

Nitroglycerin (sublingual)Nitrostat · sublingual tablets
Nitroglycerin (spray)Nitrolingual · lingual spray
Nitroglycerin (patch)Nitro-Dur · transdermal patch
Isosorbide mononitrateGeneric only · IR and ER tablets
Isosorbide dinitrateGeneric only · tablets (Isordil not listed)
RanolazineGeneric only · ER tablets (Ranexa no longer marketed)

What to know

  • Used for: sublingual tablets and spray treat an attack; patches, isosorbide and ranolazine prevent attacks.
  • Side effects: headache, dizziness, low blood pressure.
  • Nitrostat stays in its original glass bottle, tightly capped after each use, to keep its potency. The tablet dissolves under the tongue.
  • Patches have a daily off period (label: 12 to 14 hours on, 10 to 12 hours off) to prevent tolerance.
  • Look-alike names: isosorbide mononitrate and isosorbide dinitrate; they are not interchangeable.
Safety Nitrates are contraindicated with PDE-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil, avanafil) and riociguat. As-needed nitroglycerin counts. Route any such profile to the pharmacist.

What to know

GLP-1 receptor agonists

Type 2 diabetes and, under other brand names, chronic weight management

Common drugs

Semaglutide (diabetes)Ozempic · pen; Ozempic · tablets; Rybelsus · tablets
Semaglutide (weight)Wegovy · pen, tablets
DulaglutideTrulicity · single-dose pens
LiraglutideVictoza (diabetes), Saxenda (weight) · pens

With basal insulinXultophy (degludec/liraglutide), Soliqua (glargine/lixisenatide) · pens

What to know

  • Stem: -glutide (semaglutide, dulaglutide, liraglutide). Tirzepatide acts on GIP and GLP-1.
  • One molecule, different brands by use: Ozempic and Wegovy, Victoza and Saxenda, Mounjaro and Zepbound. Enter the exact product written.

What to know

Hormonal contraceptives

Prevent pregnancy: pills, patches, rings and emergency contraception

Common products

Ethinyl estradiol/drospirenoneYaz, Yasmin · tablets
Ethinyl estradiol/norethindrone acetateJunel, Junel Fe (branded generics) · tablets
Ethinyl estradiol/norgestimateSprintec (branded generic) · tablets
Progestin-only pillsCamila (norethindrone), Slynd (drospirenone) · tablets
NorgestrelOpill · tablets (OTC daily pill)
Contraceptive patchXulane, Zafemy (norelgestromin/EE); Twirla (levonorgestrel/EE)
Vaginal ringNuvaRing, EluRyng (etonogestrel/EE); Annovera (segesterone/EE)

What to know

  • Boxed warning on combined products: cigarette smoking raises the risk of serious heart and blood vessel events, more so over age 35. Xulane and Twirla are also contraindicated with a BMI of 30 or more.
  • Federal rule (21 CFR 310.501): a patient package insert goes with every oral contraceptive dispensed. In hospitals and long-term care, before the first dose and every 30 days.
  • "Fe" in a name means the last tablets contain iron instead of hormone.
  • NuvaRing and EluRyng are refrigerated until dispensed; after dispensing they keep up to 4 months at room temperature, so the label gets an expiration date no later than 4 months from dispensing (or the product expiration, if sooner).
  • Emergency contraception windows: Plan B One-Step within 72 hours; ella within 120 hours (5 days).
  • Enzyme inducers can lower contraceptive effect (Yaz label): route new interacting drugs to the pharmacist.

What a technician should know

  • Stems: -prost (prostaglandin analogs), -olol (beta blockers), -zolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitors).
  • Xalatan storage: refrigerate unopened bottles (36 to 46°F); once opened, it may stay at room temperature up to 77°F for 6 weeks. Rhopressa: refrigerate until opened, then up to 6 weeks at 36 to 77°F.
  • Prostaglandin drops can darken the iris (likely permanent), the eyelid skin and lashes, and make lashes longer and thicker.
  • Timolol drops are absorbed into the body: contraindicated in bronchial asthma (beta-blocker effects).
  • Alphagan P is contraindicated in infants under 2 years.
  • Several drops: wait at least 5 minutes between different eye drops. Remove contact lenses before Xalatan and wait 15 minutes to put them back.
  • Days supply for eye drops is estimated from the bottle size and the directions; follow the payer's drops-per-mL rule.
